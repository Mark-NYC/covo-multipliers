The real problem

The Problem May Not Be Your Commitment.

You care about making disciples and seeing churches multiply. You may already know the tools, attend trainings, pray, share the gospel, and invest in people. But when one part of the planter stays underdeveloped, the whole work can stall. Without a clear diagnosis, it is easy to answer every slowdown with more content, more activity, or more effort.

1 Vision doesn't become practice

You know the vision and the tools, but they keep struggling to turn into consistent field practice.

2 You add instead of address

More training, more meetings, more activity — without strengthening the area that is actually weak.

3 You're uneven, not unfaithful

Strong in evangelism, strategy, prayer, or leadership — but uneven across the whole work.

4 You can't name the blockage

You feel stalled but can't tell whether it's in your thinking, inner life, practice, or presence in the harvest.

The root issue is usually not effort. It's that you lack a simple, whole-person diagnostic — so you cannot address what you have not clearly named.

If nothing changes

If You Can't Name the Blockage, You'll Keep Working Around It.

Next month can look a lot like this month — the same effort, the same uncertainty, the same weak area quietly limiting everything else.

You keep learning and trying new things while the same gap holds you back.
Activity stays high, but traction stays low.
You question your calling when the issue is really one unnamed area.
Good intentions never turn into a repeatable practice.

You don't need to question your calling or simply try harder. You need an honest snapshot, a clear area to strengthen, and one repeatable step that puts it into practice.

Why this lab exists

Built From Practice, Not Theory

The Muddy Boots Church Planter Assessment comes out of ordinary, covocational disciple-making — the work of following Jesus and helping others follow him in the middle of real jobs, families, and relationships. It's the same honest, four-part check the facilitators run on their own practice. You'll use it on yours.

The mechanism

The Muddy Boots Church Planter Assessment

Effective disciple-making involves more than knowing the right tools. This assessment helps you look honestly at four connected parts of your life and practice, so you can see what needs attention next.

Part One Head

Assess whether generational disciple-making vision, biblical grounding, and the ability to learn and adjust are guiding your actions.

Part Two Heart

Examine whether love for Jesus, dependence on the Spirit, prayer, and perseverance are sustaining the work.

Part Three Hands

Look honestly at whether you are practicing reproducing methods and developing other disciple-making leaders.

Part Four Harvester

Evaluate whether your boots are actually muddy — through evangelism in your own backyard, church in everyday spaces, and labor alongside other harvesters.

A–F

About the grades: During the live lab you'll give yourself a quick A-to-F snapshot in each area. This is a private, honest snapshot — not a judgment of your worth, faithfulness, or calling. The goal is clarity and a faithful next step, not a perfect score.

In 45 minutes, you'll know what to work on next. Enough clarity to take one real step this week.
